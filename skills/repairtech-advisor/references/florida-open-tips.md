# Open tips for Central Florida cars

These are neighbor-level tips, not a diagnosis. Your owner's manual still wins.
RepairTech is in Kissimmee — heat, short trips, and stop-and-go count as **severe
service** for most makers.

## Heat
- Cabin AC works the engine and the condenser hard from April through October.
  Weak cooling, a sweet smell, or the fan screaming are reasons to have it checked
  before a long drive, not "next winter."
- Batteries cook in Florida heat. Slow crank, clicking, or a battery older than
  about four years is worth a load test — not a guess from this chat.
- Parked in sun: tire pressure rises. Check cold in the morning against the door
  sticker, not the sidewall max.

## Oil and filters
- Severe-service oil changes are closer together than the "normal" chart in the
  manual. Short trips and heat put fuel and water in the oil.
- A complimentary oil change that used cheap oil or the wrong filter is common on
  used cars here. If you have no records, start with a documented oil and filter
  service and keep the receipt.

## Rain and roads
- Florida afternoon storms: wiper blades and tires matter more than people think.
  Bald tires in a downpour are a safety item.
- Standing water: if the car ingested water (rough running after a deep puddle),
  do not keep driving it — that is a tow / inspection, not a tip.

## What this is not
- Not a substitute for the inspection, estimate, or invoice.
- Not OEM interval tables for a specific VIN. Ask the shop to pull the schedule
  for *your* car.
