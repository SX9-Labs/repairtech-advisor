# What's urgent vs what can wait

## Treat now — don't drive until the shop confirms it's safe
- **Brakes** — grinding, soft or sinking pedal, pulling when braking, brake warning light.
- **Steering / suspension** — clunks, wandering, the wheel fighting you. A separation is dangerous.
- **Fuel leaks** — raw fuel smell, visible drips. Fire hazard.
- **Overheating** — temperature gauge climbing, steam, sweet smell. Driving on it can crack the
  engine — a small repair becomes a huge one.

## Soon (days–weeks, before it grows)
- Check-engine light **flashing** (that one is actually urgent — misfire that can ruin the
  catalytic converter), battery/charging warnings, new noises that are getting worse, fluid spots
  that keep appearing.

## Can usually wait (plan it into the budget)
- Check-engine light **steady** with no drivability change, cosmetic items, wear items the shop
  measured and marked "monitor" (pads with life left, tires above the wear bar), minor seepage
  noted as "watch."

## The honest rule
If a report marks something red/safety, ask the shop one question: **"Is it safe to drive home?"**
A good shop answers that straight. RepairTech's reports separate "confirmed" from "most likely" on
purpose — you're allowed to decline or defer anything that isn't a safety item.
